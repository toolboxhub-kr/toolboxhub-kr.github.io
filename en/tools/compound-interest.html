How Compound Interest Works (Formula)

Compound interest means earning returns not only on your original deposits, but also on all accumulated prior interest. With monthly contributions, the future value formula is:

r = Annual Rate ÷ 12  |  n = Years × 12
FV = P × (1 + r)^n + PMT × [((1 + r)^n - 1) ÷ r]
Rule of 72 (Years to Double) ≈ 72 ÷ Annual Rate (%)

Practical Investing Tips

💡 Time in the market beats timing the market: Starting just 5 years earlier often outweighs doubling your monthly contribution later, because compound growth accelerates sharply in the final decade.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Rule of 72 in compound interest?
Divide 72 by your annual return percentage to estimate how many years it takes for an investment to double. At 8% per year, $10,000 doubles to $20,000 in roughly 9 years.
Should I adjust the return rate for inflation?
Yes! Subtracting ~2.5%–3% for long-term inflation (e.g., entering 7% instead of 10%) shows your future balance in today's purchasing power.
Are tax-advantaged accounts better for compounding?
Yes. Accounts like a 401(k), Roth IRA, or UK ISA shelter dividends and capital gains from annual taxes, letting more of your gains compound undisturbed.
